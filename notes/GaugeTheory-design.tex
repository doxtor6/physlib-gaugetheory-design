\section*{What the library must do}

The target is one theorem (\S1): for the Standard Model, the gauge- and Lorentz-invariant local expressions of mass dimension at most four are spanned by an explicit list. Reaching it in a way any other model can reuse needs four things. Status as of 9 November 2026.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{Describe a theory from its card.} A gauge group given as a list of factors and a table of fields with charges and generations produce everything else: the gauge data, the matter fields, the connection, and the field data of the theory. \emph{(done; the field data exists as \lean{GaugeFieldData}, to be renamed; \S3.1--3.3, \S4)}
\item \textbf{Write and classify its local expressions.} The algebra of the symbols $\partial_s\psi_\alpha$ carries the gauge and Lorentz actions, the total derivative and the mass-weight grading; a term written from the card's names can be checked for invariance, and the invariants of a given mass dimension come as a spanning list, one family of terms at a time. \emph{(done on hand-built data for the Standard Model; the generic statement and the card-level interface to do; \S3.5, \S4, \S6)}
\item \textbf{Relate theories to one another.} Two descriptions of a theory can be shown equivalent, a theory embedded in or projected onto another, or restricted along a change of gauge group, and every construction above respects these maps; sectors are the parts belonging to sub-theories. \emph{(designed; the first piece implemented, PR coming; \S3.4)}
\item \textbf{Reality, and what comes after.} Real fields and a star, so that a real Lagrangian is a star-fixed invariant; then invariants modulo total derivatives and equations of motion, symmetry breaking, flavour symmetries and higher mass dimension. \emph{(to do; the rest after the deadline; \S3.5, \S4, \S6)}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Purpose}

\lean{PhyslibAlpha/ClassicalFieldTheory/GaugeTheory/} is the symbolic framework for the local content of a gauge theory: the algebra in which a Lagrangian is written, the gauge and Lorentz actions on it, and the invariants of a given mass dimension. Fields enter only through their jets at one point, the formal symbols $\partial_s\psi_\alpha$.

One rule organises the folder: \textbf{every layer is parametrised by data and returns constructions and theorems; a concrete theory contributes only its data.} A model supplies a gauge datum and a table of matter fields, and everything else about it is inherited.

\paragraph{The target theorem.} For the Standard Model, the gauge- and Lorentz-invariant elements of the local field algebra of mass weight at most 8 (mass dimension at most 4) are spanned by an explicit list. This is a classification of invariant \emph{polynomials}: it is over $\mathbb C$, imposes no reality condition, and takes no quotient by total derivatives or equations of motion. It therefore contains terms such as $\partial^2(H^\dagger H)$ and $\operatorname{tr}F\tilde F$, and it should be named as a statement about invariants, not as ``the Lagrangian''. Reality and the quotient by total derivatives are later steps (\S6).

\paragraph{Deadline.} Early December 2026, with the end of the semester as the hard deadline.

\section{The idea}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Gauge jets.} A gauge transformation is its jet $U$ in a jet group $G_J$: value $\mathrm{eval}\,U\in G_0$, Lie algebra $\mathfrak g_J$, derivatives, adjoint action, Maurer--Cartan form $\omega_\mu(U)=i(\partial_\mu U)U^{-1}$. These maps and their laws are the gauge datum; nothing downstream knows which group it is.
\item \textbf{Matter fields.} A matter field is a finite-dimensional complex space $V$ with a Lorentz representation, an action of $G_J$ on $\mathtt{JetRing}\otimes V$ that is fibrewise and generated by an infinitesimal action of $\mathfrak g$, a mass weight and a statistic (bosonic or fermionic). Its symbols transform linearly.
\item \textbf{The gauge field.} The connection is not listed by a theory: it is determined by the gauge datum. Its symbols transform affinely, by the adjoint action plus the Maurer--Cartan form, and the translation part satisfies the cocycle law $t(UV)=t(U)+\mathrm{Ad}_U\,t(V)$. Matter and the connection are two separate notions; the affine picture describes how they differ but is not a common type.
\item \textbf{Field data.} A theory's field data is a finite family of matter fields indexed by its species, bosonic and fermionic kept apart, compiled from its table with one species per multiplet and generation; it exists as \lean{GaugeFieldData} and is to be renamed \lean{FieldData}. The family is a \emph{presentation}: what a theory is, is its merged bosonic and fermionic modules with their actions, and morphisms see only these. The connection is added from the gauge datum, so the pure gauge theory is the field data with no species, and the sum of two theories is the disjoint union of their species.
\item \textbf{Morphisms.} Equivalence, embedding and projection of models are morphisms, never equality. Over one gauge datum a morphism is an intertwiner of the merged modules, equivalently a matrix of intertwiners between species, not a bijection of species, because the split into species is not canonical when a multiplet repeats. Along a morphism of gauge data, matter fields restrict and the connection maps forward. The connection has no free data, so it transports automatically; the branching of a gauge sector (the $X$, $Y$ bosons of $SU(5)$ seen from the Standard Model) is a statement about the algebras, not about the field data.
\item \textbf{The algebra.} $J(T)$ is the free graded-commutative algebra on the symbols of the matter fields of $T$, their conjugates, and the connection. Every action is defined on generators and extended by the universal property; the translation part of the connection gives the inhomogeneous term. A morphism of field data gives an equivariant map of algebras in the opposite direction, the symbols being covectors, and sectors are images of such maps.
\item \textbf{Invariants.} Mass weight is twice the mass dimension (derivative 2, scalar 2, Weyl fermion 3, connection 2). On the covariant subalgebra jets act through $G_0$, so classification is ordinary invariant theory. It is done one family of terms at a time: a \emph{reduction} $\mathrm{ReducesInvariantsTo}\;\sigma\;V\;W$ says that for every $\sigma$-stable submodule $S$, an invariant of $V\sqcup S$ lies in $W\sqcup S$. Reductions compose, combine over finite joins and transport along maps of index sets, and $\sigma$ is any family of linear maps, so gauge and Lorentz invariance are one invariance for the disjoint union of the two groups.
\end{itemize}

Imports go down the line only. Three points decide the shape:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Two field notions, one interface per model.} Matter fields and the connection are separate: each states its own transformation laws once, and the matter laws are stated for an arbitrary \lean{MatterField}. A statement over all fields of a theory uses a type built once the model is fixed,
\begin{lstlisting}
inductive Field (T : FieldData jets)
  | boson (i : T.BosonSpecies) | fermion (i : T.FermionSpecies) | gauge
\end{lstlisting}
which lives in \lean{Type}, has decidable equality, and whose laws dispatch to the matter and connection laws. A closed type \lean{Species | matter M | gauge} at the level of data was tried (PR \#2011) and dropped: every general lemma split into the two cases, the matter case only restated existing results, and it did not say how a theory contains its gauge field.
\item \textbf{One algebra tower.} The pure gauge theory is $J$ of the field data with no matter, and the sectors of $J(T)$ are images of the maps induced by projections onto sub-theories (\S3.4). Nothing is built twice and identified afterwards.
\item \textbf{One classification mechanism.} The relation \lean{ReducesInvariantsTo} of \lean{Mathematics/InvariantReduction} is general mathematics and stays there. The algebra layer supplies what every theory needs to use it: the gauge and Lorentz actions as one family indexed by $G_0\sqcup SL(2,\mathbb C)$, the reduction of a factor's invariance to that family, and the stability of symbol ranges, all in \lean{LocalCovFieldAlgebra/}. A classification theorem for one index law (a colour trace, an isospin contraction, a Lorentz scalar) is stated as a reduction modulo an arbitrary stable submodule, so it can be applied inside any larger expression and to any extension.
\end{itemize}

Each layer is described below by its datum, what it derives and its interface.

\subsection{\texttt{LocalGaugeData/}: the gauge datum}

\paragraph{The question it answers.} A Lagrangian density at a point $x$ depends on the fields and finitely many of their derivatives at $x$. So when we ask whether it is gauge invariant, a gauge transformation $g(x)$ matters only through its Taylor series at that point, its \emph{jet}. This layer describes these jets and nothing else. It does not mention fields or Lagrangians; every later layer takes its datum as input.

\paragraph{An example first.} For $U(1)$, a gauge transformation is $u(x)=e^{i\theta(x)}$. Its jet is the power series of $u$ in the coordinates $x_0,\dots,x_3$, a unitary element of $\mathbb C[[x]]$. Its value at the base point is $u(0)\in U(1)$. Its \emph{Maurer--Cartan form} $\omega_\mu=i(\partial_\mu u)u^{-1}=-\partial_\mu\theta$ is exactly the shift of the gauge field, $A_\mu\mapsto A_\mu+\omega_\mu$. For $SU(n)$ everything is the same with $n\times n$ matrices of power series, and $\omega_\mu=i(\partial_\mu U)U^{-1}$ is a traceless hermitian matrix.

\paragraph{The datum.} \lean{LocalGaugeData G₀ }$\mathfrak g$\lean{ GJ }$\mathfrak g$\lean{J} abstracts the example. It has four types: the gauge group $G_0$ and its Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$, and their jet versions $G_J$ and $\mathfrak g_J$. It has the maps a transformation law uses:
\begin{itemize}
\item evaluation at the base point and constant jets, $G_J\leftrightarrows G_0$ and $\mathfrak g_J\leftrightarrows\mathfrak g$;
\item derivatives $\partial_\mu$ and multiplication by coordinates $x_\mu$ on $\mathfrak g_J$;
\item the adjoint action $\mathrm{Ad}_U$ of a gauge jet on Lie algebra jets;
\item the Maurer--Cartan form $\omega_\mu(U)$.
\end{itemize}
It also has the rules these satisfy in the example, such as $\omega(UV)=\omega(U)+\mathrm{Ad}_U\,\omega(V)$ and $\partial_\mu\omega_\nu-\partial_\nu\omega_\mu+[\omega_\mu,\omega_\nu]=0$. Everything later is proved from these rules alone, so it holds for every gauge group at once.

\paragraph{What is proved from the rules.} Three tools that later layers need:
\begin{itemize}
\item how $\mathrm{Ad}_U$ acts on Taylor coefficients (a Leibniz formula), which is how a gauge jet moves the derivatives of a field;
\item the \emph{symmetrised} Maurer--Cartan data, a smaller set of numbers from which all Taylor coefficients of $\omega$ can be recovered;
\item the jets that agree with the identity up to order $n$; these form normal subgroups, and such a jet only shifts the first $n$ derivatives of the gauge field.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Two extra properties.} The rules do not say that $G_J$ really consists of Taylor series, so two properties are stated separately. \lean{Faithful}: a jet is determined by its Taylor coefficients. \lean{Free}: every choice of Taylor coefficients comes from some jet. \lean{Free} is what later shows that the gauge field enters an invariant only through its field strength: gauge jets are rich enough to remove every other combination of derivatives of $A_\mu$.

\paragraph{Where concrete groups come from.} A \emph{matrix presentation} (\lean{MatrixJets}) says that the four types sit inside matrices and every map is the matrix one. From it the rules and \lean{Faithful} are proved once, and \lean{Free} is reduced to three conditions on the carriers, so $SU(n)$ (\lean{su n}) and $U(1)$ (\lean{u1}, as $1\times1$ matrices) are obtained by filling in a presentation. Products (\lean{prod}) and a list of factors (\lean{ofFactors}) then give any group such as $SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)$, written \lean{[.SU 3, .SU 2, .U1]}.

\paragraph{Factors and morphisms.} Charges are given one factor at a time, for example ``a doublet of $SU(2)$ with hypercharge $-3$''. A \emph{factor} (\lean{U1Factor}, \lean{SUFactor}) records how the gauge jets project onto one $U(1)$ or $SU(n)$. A \emph{morphism} (\lean{Hom}) is a map between two data that respects all the maps above, and a factor of the target pulls back along it. The projections of a product are morphisms, which is how the factors of $SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)$ are obtained from those of its pieces.

\paragraph{Conventions.} The datum is always passed explicitly, never found by instance search, because the four types do not determine it. The layer imports nothing from the layers above it.

\subsection{\texttt{MatterField/} and \texttt{GaugeBoson/}: the two kinds of field}

\paragraph{Matter.} \lean{MatterField jets} has a complex value space $V$ (free and finite), a Lorentz representation, the jet action \lean{repJet} on $\mathtt{JetRing}\otimes V$, required to be fibrewise and generated by the infinitesimal action \lean{repAlgebra}, a mass weight and a statistic (PR \#1989). Matrix representations and tables (\lean{MatrixRep}) produce matter fields from charges one factor at a time and prove the infinitesimal-action condition once for all of them. The coefficients by which a jet moves the derivatives of a field live in \lean{RepCoeff}, separate from the covariant derivative, so that field data can use them without importing the algebra.

\paragraph{The connection.} The value space is $\mathrm{CoVector}\otimes\mathfrak g$, real. The coefficients are the adjoint transport and the Maurer--Cartan coefficients (\lean{GaugeCoeff}), with their composition laws: the linear part composes by a Leibniz sum, and the translation part is a cocycle. The connection is functorial in the gauge datum (\S3.4).

\paragraph{Requirements from the classification.} Keep the value-space representation distinct from the dual action on field symbols, including their conjugates. Parity and mass weight belong to the field; bases and particular fundamental, conjugate or adjoint representations are inputs to specific classifiers, not requirements on every field. Keep the conditions behind covariance visible: \lean{GaugeLorentzCompatible} supplies Lorentz stability of covariant expressions, and \lean{PureJetsActTrivially} makes their jet action factor through evaluation. Whether these become fields of \lean{MatterField}, discharged once for matrix representations, or remain hypotheses on the field data is open (\S5). The converse, that jet-invariant expressions lie in the covariant algebra, uses \lean{Free} and is a separate result.

\subsection{\texttt{FieldData/}: what a theory contains}

\paragraph{The datum.} The field data of a theory is the indexed family that exists today as \lean{GaugeFieldData}: species types \lean{FermionSpecies} and \lean{BosonSpecies}, stored as fields of the structure with \lean{Fintype} and \lean{DecidableEq}, a \lean{MatterField} for each species with the matching statistic, and the gauge field implicit. It is to be renamed \lean{FieldData}, and the table layer's \lean{LocalGaugeData.FieldData Γ Fields} then needs another name, such as \lean{FieldTable}. The table compiles to it with species \lean{Σ f, Fin (generations f)}, so names and generation counts stay where a Lagrangian is written, with Yukawa matrices indexed by \lean{Fin 3 × Fin 3} per fermion type. All of \lean{LocalFieldAlgebra/} and \lean{LocalCovFieldAlgebra/} is built on it.

\paragraph{Presentation and meaning.} The species are a choice. When a multiplet occurs more than once, as the three generations of each fermion or the two doublets of a two-Higgs-doublet model, splitting the theory into species is a choice of basis of the multiplicity space, and descriptions related by a change of that basis, a flavour rotation or a change of Higgs basis, are the same theory. What does not depend on the choice is the merged content: the \emph{fermionic module} \lean{T.FermionModule} $=\prod_i V_i$ and the \emph{bosonic module} \lean{T.BosonModule}, with their Lorentz and jet actions and their mass-weight grading. These exist today and need no common mass weight. So the indexed family is the datum, because the algebra and the classification work species by species, and the merged modules are what morphisms see (\S3.4). Both positions of 5 November were partly right: the morphisms belong to the merged content, the datum to the indexed family.

\paragraph{The merged alternative.} On 5 November the thread chose instead one bosonic and one fermionic \lean{MatterField}, merged by \lean{MatterField.prod} and \lean{pi}, because their inclusions and projections give embeddings directly. On 9 November Nathaneal found that \lean{GaugeFieldData} is the indexed design and that the algebra is built on it, and reopened the question. As the datum, merged fields would need the algebra rebuilt and would forget the decomposition into multiplets that the classification uses. They also force one mass weight per statistic, which is mild: spinors have weight 3, scalars and vectors weight 2, and only fields such as supersymmetric auxiliary fields break it. The merged fields remain available as \lean{bosonMatterField} and \lean{fermionMatterField}, under a common-weight hypothesis.

\paragraph{Still to do.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Individual multiplets.} An accessor is the inclusion or projection of one species in the merged module, \lean{inclBosonValue} and \lean{projBosonValue} and their fermionic versions, which exist; as morphisms they form a retract (\S3.4).
\item \emph{The empty case.} Empty species types; the pure gauge theory is the field data with no species.
\item \emph{Compatibility conditions.} \lean{GaugeLorentzCompatible} and \lean{PureJetsActTrivially} are stated species by species; the table layer proves the first for every compiled field.
\end{itemize}
Fields with an affine shift other than the connection (Stückelberg, axion), if ever needed, get their own family with an affine datum.

\subsection{Morphisms: equivalence, embedding, projection}

Joseph asked (4 November) that one model can be embedded in or projected onto another, and that two descriptions of one model can be shown equivalent. None of this is equality. \lean{MatterField} carries a type $V$, so two isomorphic fields are never equal in Lean, whatever the shape of the field data; and a change of gauge group changes the type of the field data. Both are handled by morphisms, and the gauge side already has them: \lean{LocalGaugeData.Hom} in \lean{Physlib/}, with \lean{id} and \lean{comp}, and in \lean{PhyslibAlpha/} the projections \lean{Hom.fst}, \lean{Hom.snd} and \lean{Factor.comap}. The anomaly-cancellation library sets the house style in \lean{ACCSystem.Hom}: the data of a map plus one commuting condition. What is missing is the matter side and the connection, three definitions.

\paragraph{Intertwiners, over one gauge datum.} \lean{MatterField.Hom M N} is a $\mathbb C$-linear map $V_M\to V_N$ commuting with the Lorentz action, the jet action (through $\mathtt{JetRing}\otimes-$) and the gauge-algebra action, with equal mass weight and statistic. It was agreed on 6 November and Nathaneal has implemented it, with the quiver notation $M\longrightarrow N$ and no category instance, as Joseph preferred. With \lean{id} and \lean{comp} it composes; \lean{Equiv} is an isomorphism; an embedding is an injective morphism and a projection a surjective one, packaged as a retract $(\iota,\pi)$ with $\pi\circ\iota=\mathrm{id}$. \lean{prod} is a biproduct and \lean{pi} a finite one: \lean{inl}, \lean{inr}, \lean{fst}, \lean{snd}, \lean{proj i}, \lean{single i} are morphisms, with \lean{lift} and \lean{desc}. The inclusion and projection of one species in the merged module are of this kind, and form a retract. The flavour group comes for free: a $3\times3$ matrix on \lean{pi (fun _ : Fin 3 => M)} is an endomorphism, an invertible one an automorphism, the unitary ones being the flavour rotations once a hermitian structure is added, and ``three copies of $M$ are $M\times(M\times M)$'' is an \lean{Equiv}.

\paragraph{Restriction, along a morphism of gauge data.} For a morphism of gauge data \lean{φ : Hom jets jets'}, \lean{MatterField.comap φ} sends a matter field over \lean{jets'} to one over \lean{jets}: the same $V$ and Lorentz action, the jet action composed with \lean{φ.groupJ}, the algebra action composed with \lean{φ.lie}. The infinitesimal-action law is stated through \lean{deriv}, \lean{mulCoord}, \lean{adjointJ}, \lean{maurerCartan} and \lean{evalLie}, and \lean{Hom} commutes with each of these, so the law transports; those compatibility fields exist for this. It is contravariant, like \lean{Factor.comap}, and compiling a pulled-back table row should agree with pulling back the compiled field, up to \lean{Equiv}.

\paragraph{The connection, along a morphism of gauge data.} The connection has no free data, so it needs no morphism type of its own, only functoriality. On values, \lean{GaugeBoson.map φ} is $\mathrm{id}\otimes\varphi_{\mathfrak g}$ from $\mathrm{CoVector}\otimes\mathfrak g$ to $\mathrm{CoVector}\otimes\mathfrak g'$. The symbols of the gauge field are covectors, so on the jet algebras the induced map runs the other way, \lean{LocalGaugeFieldAlgebra.comap φ} from the algebra of the $\mathfrak g'$ gauge field to that of the $\mathfrak g$ gauge field: an expression in the larger gauge field is evaluated on fields valued in the smaller algebra. It is equivariant, with $G_J$ acting on the source through \lean{φ.groupJ}, because \lean{φ.lieJ_adjointJ} carries the adjoint transport, and \lean{φ.lieJ_maurerCartan} the Maurer--Cartan coefficient, to those of \lean{φ.groupJ U}; the Lorentz action is untouched. This is what the physics says: a Standard Model gauge field is an $SU(5)$ gauge field through $\mathfrak g\subset\mathfrak g'$, so any $SU(5)$ expression can be evaluated on it.

\paragraph{Morphisms of models.} Over one gauge datum, a morphism $T\to T'$ is a pair of linear maps of the merged modules, commuting with every action and preserving the grading:
\begin{lstlisting}
structure FieldData.Hom (T T' : FieldData jets) where
  fermion : T.FermionModule @$\to_{\ell}[\mathbb{C}]$@ T'.FermionModule
  boson   : T.BosonModule @$\to_{\ell}[\mathbb{C}]$@ T'.BosonModule
  -- commuting with the Lorentz, jet and gauge-algebra actions,
  -- and with the mass-weight scaling
\end{lstlisting}
Equivalently it is a matrix of \lean{MatterField.Hom}s between species of equal weight. An equivalence is an invertible one, an embedding an injective one, a projection a surjective one; renaming rows, listing generations separately and changing basis are all equivalences. A bijection of species with an isomorphism on each is a special case, and too strict to be the definition: it would make a flavour rotation, or a change of Higgs basis, a different theory. The test is the automorphism group, which should be the global symmetries commuting with gauge and Lorentz transformations. For the Standard Model fermions, five pairwise distinct irreducible multiplets each occurring three times, Schur's lemma gives $GL(3,\mathbb C)^5$, whose unitary part $U(3)^5$ is the flavour group once a hermitian structure is added (\S3.5); species bijections give only permutations of generations and rescalings of each species.

Along a morphism of gauge data $\varphi$, a morphism from $T$ to $T'$ is a morphism from $T$ to $T'$ restricted along $\varphi$, species by species, the connection being carried by \lean{GaugeBoson.map φ}. Along an isomorphism $\varphi$ this gives equivalence up to an automorphism of the gauge datum, such as a rescaling of hypercharge or charge conjugation. Embedding the Standard Model in $SU(5)$ is an equivalence between the Standard Model data and the $SU(5)$ data restricted along the block embedding; projecting onto one factor is restriction along \lean{Hom.fst}.

At the level of the algebras the direction reverses, because the symbols are covectors: a morphism $T\to T'$ induces an equivariant algebra map $J(T')\to J(T)$, from \lean{JetComponentSpace.comap}, which exists, on each generator space and \lean{LocalGaugeFieldAlgebra.comap} on the connection. For an embedding it restricts an expression to the sub-theory, setting the other fields to zero; for a projection it is injective, and its image is the sector of the sub-theory. This functoriality theorem is what makes the data-level notion trustworthy, and it is how sectors arise.

\paragraph{Branching of a gauge sector lives at the algebra level.} Along $\varphi:\mathfrak g\hookrightarrow\mathfrak g'$ with $\mathfrak g'=\mathfrak g\oplus\mathfrak m$ as $\mathfrak g$-modules, the $\mathfrak g'$ gauge field seen over \lean{jets} is not a gauge field of \lean{jets}. It is the \lean{jets} gauge field, transforming affinely, together with a field valued in $\mathrm{CoVector}\otimes\mathfrak m$ transforming homogeneously, because $\omega(\varphi U)$ lands in $\mathfrak g$. These are the $X$ and $Y$ bosons, a bosonic \emph{matter} field in $(3,2)_{-5}\oplus(\bar 3,2)_{5}$ with a Lorentz-vector label. A map of field data sends gauge to gauge, so this is not one; it is an isomorphism of algebras, the algebra of the $\mathfrak g'$ gauge field with $G_J$ acting through $\varphi$ against the algebra of the $\mathfrak g$ gauge field tensored with $J$ of the coset field. It needs a vector Lorentz label and complexified real fields, neither of which the table has (\S4), and it is the reason not to promise projections of models at the data level alone. This refines item 5 of \S4: restriction along \lean{Hom} branches every matter field; a gauge sector branches at the algebra.

The rename of \lean{GaugeFieldData} is independent of all of these. The open TODO on \lean{LocalGaugeData.Hom}, a constructor from a Lie group homomorphism, is what the $SU(5)$ embedding needs.

\subsection{\texttt{LocalFieldAlgebra/} and \texttt{LocalCovFieldAlgebra/}: the algebras}

Each directory is named after the algebra it defines, \lean{LocalFieldAlgebra} ($J(T)$) and \lean{LocalCovFieldAlgebra}; today they are \lean{LocalField/} and \lean{LocalCovField/}.

$J(T)$ is built from the per-field generator spaces, bosonic generators commuting and fermionic ones anticommuting, with the connection's generators complexified. Its universal property says that every compatible assignment of the symbols to an associative complex algebra $B$ extends to exactly one algebra map; $B$ need not be commutative and the map need not be injective. Gauge and Lorentz actions, ordinary derivatives and mass weight are defined on generators. The current sectors use only the totals per category (fermion, gauge, scalar); sectors by sub-families follow from the maps induced by projections onto sub-theories (\S3.4).

The covariant subalgebra is generated by the matter fields, their symmetrised covariant derivatives and the covariant derivatives of the field strength; on it the jets act through $G_0$. Each sector of the classification supplies a reduction, containment of its classified span in the source, and pointwise fixedness of that span; these combine without sector independence.

\paragraph{Conjugates and reality.} The conjugate components already sit in each field's jet component space. Still to decide (\S5): a real/complex flag per matter field, conjugate symbols generated and never listed, and a star structure on $J(T)$ that swaps a symbol with its conjugate and fixes real fields and the connection. A real Lagrangian is then a star-fixed invariant.

\section{What a model sees}

A model is a card and nothing else. The Standard Model card (\lean{PhyslibAlpha/Particles/StandardModel/Basic.lean}) reads:
\begin{lstlisting}
abbrev gauge : List FactorSpec := [.SU 3, .SU 2, .U1]
abbrev leptonDoublet : MatterFieldData factors := (.L, .singlet, .fund, -3)
abbrev higgs : MatterFieldData factors := (.scalar, .singlet, .fund, 3)
inductive Fields | q | l | u | d | e | H
def table : FieldData factors Fields | .q => (3, quarkDoublet) | ...
\end{lstlisting}
The fields of the model are abbreviations of the value spaces the card produces, and theorems are stated in the card and the generic vocabulary alone. This is the interface of model-building tools such as SARAH and Sym2Int: the gauge group and a list of matter fields with generations, the gauge fields never listed. To complete it:
\begin{enumerate}
\item real fields: a real-scalar label or a reality flag (\lean{LorentzLabel} has only \lean{L}, \lean{R}, \lean{scalar}, all complex), and a vector label, which a branched gauge sector needs (\S3.4);
\item a gauge field and a field strength for each gauge factor, obtained from the factor projections, with names the card can use;
\item conjugates generated automatically, through the star structure and a user-facing conjugation;
\item writing a term from the card's symbols and checking its invariance (the realization of symbols in $J(T)$);
\item symmetry breaking (vacuum expectation values, mass eigenstates; restriction along a \lean{Hom} branches each matter field, and a gauge sector branches at the algebra, \S3.4) and global or discrete symmetries, including the flavour group acting on generations, which \S3.4 gives as automorphisms of the field data.
\end{enumerate}
Items 1--3 belong with the field-data work; item 4 is the realization PR; item 5 comes after the deadline.
