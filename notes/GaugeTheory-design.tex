\section*{Decisions of November 2026}

\paragraph{Agreed} (Zulip, 2 November, Joseph and Nathaneal; PR \#2011 to be reworked accordingly).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{No species type.} Matter fields and the gauge field stay separate notions: \lean{MatterField} (which carries its statistic since PR \#1989) for matter, and the connection determined by \lean{LocalGaugeData}. A closed type \lean{Species | matter M | gauge} was tried in PR \#2011; every general lemma split into the two cases, the matter case only re-stated existing results, and it did not say how a theory's field content contains a gauge field. This answers Q1: two notions.
\item \textbf{The connection is implicit.} A theory lists its matter fields only; the gauge field comes from the gauge datum. This answers Q3. Sums of field contents become disjoint unions of matter families, so the gluing along the connection is no longer needed, and the pure gauge theory is the field data with no matter.
\item \textbf{The layers} are \lean{LocalGaugeData} $\to$ \lean{MatterField} $\parallel$ \lean{GaugeBoson} $\to$ \lean{FieldData} $\to$ \lean{LocalFieldAlgebra} $\to$ \lean{LocalCovFieldAlgebra}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Proposed here} (to be agreed).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{\lean{FieldData} is the indexed family that exists today}, \lean{GaugeFieldData}: index types \lean{FermionSpecies} and \lean{BosonSpecies} with \lean{Fintype} and \lean{DecidableEq}, a \lean{MatterField} for each index, the gauge field implicit. It may be renamed \lean{FieldData}. The table on the model card compiles into it.
\item \textbf{Merged fields are a derived view.} The single fermionic and bosonic fields built with \lean{MatterField.prod}/\lean{pi} are not the definition: they force one mass weight per statistic, forget the names and generations (two copies of one multiplet, as in a two-Higgs-doublet model, cannot be told apart), have no empty case, and no lemma yet shows the compatibility conditions survive them. The common-weight hypothesis is supplied where the merged field is used. A \lean{Multiset MatterField} is rejected because \lean{MatterField} contains a type and has no decidable equality.
\item \textbf{A common interface only after the model is fixed}: \lean{inductive Field T | matter (i : T.Idx) | gauge}, in \lean{Type} with decidable equality, whose laws dispatch to \lean{TransformsIn} for matter and to a connection law for the gauge field. The gauge-field results of PR \#2011 (composition laws of \lean{adjointTransport} and \lean{mcBosonCoeff}, the component form of the gauge law) move to \lean{GaugeBoson/GaugeCoeff}.
\item \textbf{Fields with an affine shift other than the connection} (Stückelberg, axion), if ever needed, get their own family with an affine datum, rather than redesigning matter now.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Open; to settle before the realization PR.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Conjugates and reality.} The conjugate components already sit in \lean{JetComponentSpace}. Decide whether each matter field carries a real/complex flag, conjugate symbols are generated and never listed, and a star structure on $J(T)$ swaps a symbol with its conjugate and fixes real fields and the connection.
\item \textbf{Compatibility.} Whether \lean{GaugeLorentzCompatible} and \lean{PureJetsActTrivially} become fields of \lean{MatterField}, discharged once for matrix representations, or remain hypotheses on the field data.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Scope and deadline.} Target early December 2026, hard deadline the end of the semester. The main theorem classifies gauge- and Lorentz-invariant \emph{polynomials} of weight at most 8: complex, with no reality condition and no quotient by total derivatives or equations of motion, so it contains terms such as $\partial^2(H^\dagger H)$ and $\operatorname{tr}F\tilde F$; it should be named accordingly rather than ``the Lagrangian''. A total-derivative derivation, reality, and the replacement of the real connection tower (step 4 of \S6) come after the deadline.

\section{Purpose}

\lean{Physlib/ClassicalFieldTheory/GaugeTheory/} is the symbolic framework for the local content of a gauge theory: the algebra in which a Lagrangian is written, the gauge and Lorentz actions on it, and the invariants of a given mass dimension. Fields enter only through their jets at one point, the formal symbols $\partial_s\psi_\alpha$.

One rule organises the folder: \textbf{every layer takes a datum and returns constructions and theorems; a concrete theory contributes only its data.} The Standard Model is a list of gauge factors and a table of fields, and everything else about it is inherited. This note records the intended structure. The present tree differs from it (\S6), and the open choices are in \S5.

\section{The idea}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Gauge jets.} A gauge transformation is its jet $U$ in a jet group $G_J$: value $\mathrm{eval}\,U\in G_0$, Lie algebra $\mathfrak g_J$, derivatives, adjoint action, Maurer--Cartan form $\omega_\mu(U)=i(\partial_\mu U)U^{-1}$. These maps and their laws are the gauge datum; nothing downstream knows which group it is.
\item \textbf{Species.} A species is a finite-dimensional $V$ with a Lorentz representation, a parity, a mass weight, and an affine action of $G_J$ on $\mathtt{JetRing}\otimes V$, a linear part $\ell$ and a translation $t$ with $t(UV)=t(U)+\ell(U)\,t(V)$. Matter has $t=0$ and $\ell$ fibrewise, generated by an infinitesimal action of $\mathfrak g$. The connection has $\ell=\mathrm{Ad}$, $t=\omega$. \dec{Superseded: there is no species type. The affine description remains the right way to think about the two cases, and the cocycle law is a lemma about the connection's coefficients, but matter (\lean{MatterField}) and the connection (from the gauge datum) are separate notions.}
\item \textbf{The algebra.} A content $T$ is a finite family of species containing the connection. \dec{Now: the field data $T$ is a finite indexed family of matter fields; the connection is added from the gauge datum.} $J(T)$ is the free graded-commutative algebra on the symbols of all species. Every action is defined on generators and extended by the universal property; the translation part gives the inhomogeneous term. Reality is a star structure. An inclusion of contents $S\subseteq T$ gives an injective equivariant map $J(S)\to J(T)$ with a retraction: sectors are its images, and the pure gauge theory is the connection-only content. \new{Contents also add: $F+G$ is the union of the two families glued along the connection, and $J(F+G)$ is the graded tensor product of $J(F)$ and $J(G)$ over the connection-only algebra, graded by which summands a word touches; both actions preserve the grading.}
\item \textbf{Invariants.} Mass weight is twice the mass dimension (derivative 2, scalar 2, Weyl fermion 3, connection 2); the dimension-four Lagrangian is the gauge and Lorentz invariants of weight at most 8. On the covariant subalgebra jets act through $G_0$, so classification is ordinary invariant theory, sector by sector. \new{It is done one family of terms at a time: a \emph{reduction} $\mathrm{ReducesInvariantsTo}\;\sigma\;V\;W$ says that for every $\sigma$-stable submodule $S$, an invariant of $V\sqcup S$ lies in $W\sqcup S$. Reductions compose, combine over finite joins and transport along maps of index sets, and the family $\sigma$ is any family of linear maps, so gauge and Lorentz invariance are one invariance for the disjoint union of the two groups. A classification of one family is a reduction of it to the span of its allowed contractions, proved in the quotient by the remainder. \textcolor{red}{When the pure and mixed pieces span the relevant weight space and satisfy the stability and reduction hypotheses, their classifications combine. Extending a classified theory requires the new-field and mixed classifications; only if the added sector is already classified does just the mixed calculation remain.}}
\end{itemize}

Imports go down the line only. Each layer exposes one structure as its datum, \lean{LocalGaugeData}, \lean{Species}, \lean{FieldContent}, and proves everything for an arbitrary instance. Two points \new{(now three)} decide the shape:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{One species notion.} Matter fields and the connection are both species, so the jet component space, the transformation law of a symbol family and the realization notion are stated once. The factors of a gauge datum belong to the gauge layer, not to matter. \dec{Superseded: two notions. Laws are stated once per notion, and a common statement over all fields is available only for a fixed model, through \lean{Field T}.}
\item \textbf{One algebra tower.} The pure gauge theory is $J$ of the connection-only content, and the sectors of $J(T)$ are images of sub-contents. Nothing is built twice and identified afterwards.
\item \new{\textbf{One classification mechanism.} The relation \lean{ReducesInvariantsTo} of \lean{Mathematics/InvariantReduction} is general mathematics and stays there. The algebra layer supplies what every theory needs to use it: the gauge and Lorentz actions as one family indexed by $G_0\sqcup SL(2,\mathbb C)$, the reduction of a factor's invariance to that family, and the stability of symbol ranges. A classification theorem for one index law (a colour trace, an isospin contraction, a Lorentz scalar) is stated as a reduction modulo an arbitrary stable submodule, so it can be applied inside any larger expression and to any extension.}
\end{itemize}

Each layer is described below by its datum, what it derives, its instances and its interface.

\subsection{\texttt{LocalGaugeData/}: the gauge datum}

\paragraph{The question it answers.} A Lagrangian density at a point $x$ depends on the fields and finitely many of their derivatives at $x$. So when we ask whether it is gauge invariant, a gauge transformation $g(x)$ matters only through its Taylor series at that point, its \emph{jet}. This layer describes these jets and nothing else. It does not mention fields or Lagrangians; every later layer takes its datum as input.

\paragraph{An example first.} For $U(1)$, a gauge transformation is $u(x)=e^{i\theta(x)}$. Its jet is the power series of $u$ in the coordinates $x_0,\dots,x_3$, a unitary element of $\mathbb C[[x]]$. Its value at the base point is $u(0)\in U(1)$. Its \emph{Maurer--Cartan form} $\omega_\mu=i(\partial_\mu u)u^{-1}=-\partial_\mu\theta$ is exactly the shift of the gauge field, $A_\mu\mapsto A_\mu+\omega_\mu$. For $SU(n)$ everything is the same with $n\times n$ matrices of power series, and $\omega_\mu=i(\partial_\mu U)U^{-1}$ is a traceless hermitian matrix.

\paragraph{The datum.} \lean{LocalGaugeData G₀ }$\mathfrak g$\lean{ GJ }$\mathfrak g$\lean{J} abstracts the example. It has four types: the gauge group $G_0$ and its Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$, and their jet versions $G_J$ and $\mathfrak g_J$. It has the maps a transformation law uses:
\begin{itemize}
\item evaluation at the base point and constant jets, $G_J\leftrightarrows G_0$ and $\mathfrak g_J\leftrightarrows\mathfrak g$;
\item derivatives $\partial_\mu$ and multiplication by coordinates $x_\mu$ on $\mathfrak g_J$;
\item the adjoint action $\mathrm{Ad}_U$ of a gauge jet on Lie algebra jets;
\item the Maurer--Cartan form $\omega_\mu(U)$.
\end{itemize}
It also has the rules these satisfy in the example, such as $\omega(UV)=\omega(U)+\mathrm{Ad}_U\,\omega(V)$ and $\partial_\mu\omega_\nu-\partial_\nu\omega_\mu+[\omega_\mu,\omega_\nu]=0$. Everything later is proved from these rules alone, so it holds for every gauge group at once.

\paragraph{What is proved from the rules.} Three tools that later layers need:
\begin{itemize}
\item how $\mathrm{Ad}_U$ acts on Taylor coefficients (a Leibniz formula), which is how a gauge jet moves the derivatives of a field;
\item the \emph{symmetrised} Maurer--Cartan data, a smaller set of numbers from which all Taylor coefficients of $\omega$ can be recovered;
\item the jets that agree with the identity up to order $n$; these form normal subgroups, and such a jet only shifts the first $n$ derivatives of the gauge field.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Two extra properties.} The rules do not say that $G_J$ really consists of Taylor series, so two properties are stated separately. \lean{Faithful}: a jet is determined by its Taylor coefficients. \lean{Free}: every choice of Taylor coefficients comes from some jet. \lean{Free} is what later shows that the gauge field enters an invariant only through its field strength: gauge jets are rich enough to remove every other combination of derivatives of $A_\mu$.

\paragraph{Where concrete groups come from.} A \emph{matrix presentation} (\lean{MatrixJets}) says that the four types sit inside matrices and every map is the matrix one. From it the rules, \lean{Faithful} and a test for \lean{Free} are proved once, so $SU(n)$ (\lean{su n}) and $U(1)$ (\lean{u1}, as $1\times1$ matrices) are obtained by filling in a presentation. Products (\lean{prod}) and a list of factors (\lean{ofFactors}) then give any group such as $SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)$, written \lean{[.SU 3, .SU 2, .U1]}.

\paragraph{Factors and morphisms.} Charges are given one factor at a time, for example ``a doublet of $SU(2)$ with hypercharge $-3$''. A \emph{factor} (\lean{U1Factor}, \lean{SUFactor}) records how the gauge jets project onto one $U(1)$ or $SU(n)$. A \emph{morphism} (\lean{Hom}) is a map between two data that respects all the maps above, and a factor of the target pulls back along it. The projections of a product are morphisms, which is how the factors of $SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)$ are obtained from those of its pieces.

\paragraph{Conventions.} The datum is always passed explicitly, never found by instance search, because the four types do not determine it. The layer imports nothing from the layers above it.
\endgroup

\subsection*{Species: requirements from the classification}
\noindent\dec{These requirements now apply to \lean{MatterField} and \lean{FieldData}, since there is no species type.}

\paragraph{Fields and symbols.} Keep the value-space representation distinct from the dual action on field symbols, including their conjugates. Parity and mass weight belong to the species; bases and particular fundamental, conjugate or adjoint representations are inputs to specific classifiers, not requirements on every species.

\paragraph{Compatibility.} Keep the conditions behind covariance visible. The current \lean{GaugeLorentzCompatible} supplies Lorentz stability of covariant expressions; \lean{PureJetsActTrivially} makes their jet action factor through evaluation. Whether these become consequences of the new species definition or remain hypotheses must be explicit. The converse statement, that jet-invariant expressions lie in the covariant algebra, uses gauge freeness and is a separate result.

\paragraph{What the assembly needs.} Each sector supplies a reduction, containment of its classified span in the source, and pointwise fixedness of that span. These combine without sector independence. Covariance and weight laws should be derived from species data and exposed to realizations. The established classification spans complex formal expressions; it imposes no reality condition and takes no quotient by total derivatives or equations of motion.

\paragraph{Migration boundary.} Begin the SM realization migration once the species-to-field-content interface, the table compiler and their algebra/realization interfaces are stable. Not every species theorem must be finished first. The shared reduction mathematics is independent of this packaging; duplicate SM interfaces can be removed as their consumers migrate.

\section{What a model sees}

A model is a card and nothing else:
\begin{lstlisting}
abbrev gauge : List FactorSpec := [.SU 3, .SU 2, .U1]
abbrev leptonDoublet : SpeciesData factors := (.L, .singlet, .fund, -3)
...
noncomputable def content : FieldContent gaugeData := table.toFieldContent
\end{lstlisting}
Species of the model are abbreviations of the value spaces the card produces, and theorems are stated in the card and the generic vocabulary alone, for example that the gauge and Lorentz invariants of \lean{content} of weight at most 4 are spanned by $1$ and $H^\dagger H$.

\section{Questions for discussion}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{One species structure or two.} The affine law costs a translation field that is zero for all matter. Is the uniformity worth it, or should \lean{MatterField} and the connection stay separate with the symbol-family law stated twice? \dec{Decided: two (2 November). \subsection*{\new{\lean{LocalGaugeData/} (24 September 2026, 9 commits for PR \#1415)}}

The factor types \lean{U1Factor}, \lean{SUFactor}, \lean{Factor}, \lean{Factors} moved down from the matrix-representation and table files into a new \lean{Factor.lean}, since their fields mention only gauge jets, the gauge algebra and the jet ring. \lean{TransformsIn} and \lean{InfinitesimalAction}, which are statements about a representation on $\mathtt{JetRing}\otimes V$, moved up to \lean{MatterField/}. The carriers of a list of factors were renamed from \lean{Factors.G} to \lean{OfFactors.G}, so that \lean{Factors} names only the type. The duplication between \lean{U1.lean} and \lean{SU/Basic.lean} was removed by a new \lean{MatrixJets.lean}: a presentation of gauge jets by matrices of jets (injective maps of the four carriers into $\kappa\times\kappa$ matrices, and what each structure map is in matrices), from which the laws of \lean{LocalGaugeData}, faithfulness and the canonical \lean{SU}-type factor are proved once; the matrix identities over the jet ring it needs went to \lean{Relativity/JetRing/Matrix}. \lean{su n} and \lean{u1} are now instances, \lean{u1} reading a scalar as a $1\times1$ matrix. Eight lemmas referenced nowhere, a stale TODO and a helper definition were removed, and the TODO on the recursive carriers was replaced by the reason for the recursion.}

\new{\emph{Now.} Eleven files, about 3\,500 lines, importing nothing outside the layer. \lean{Basic} (the structure, its laws, the iterated derivative, \lean{Faithful}); \lean{AdjointCoeff} (the Taylor--Leibniz theorem); \lean{MaurerCartan} (the symmetrised form and what determines the form); \lean{Truncation} (the filtration, pure jets, \lean{Free}); \lean{Factor} (the factor types); \lean{MatrixJets} (the presentation by matrices); \lean{SU/Algebra}, \lean{SU/Basic}, \lean{U1} (the concrete packages as instances); \lean{Prod} (products and lifting factors); \lean{OfFactors} (a list of factor symbols, its carriers and canonical factors).}

\new{\emph{Follow-up (Joseph, 25 September).} Freeness is now also proved once for a matrix presentation: \lean{MatrixJets.free} reduces \lean{Free} to three conditions on the carriers (Lie algebra jets contain every matrix with Taylor data in $\mathfrak g$, they are hermitian, and the gauge jets contain the unitary Euler transport of every Lie algebra jet), using the Euler-transport results on matrices of jets. The Standard Model freeness proof becomes an instance and shrank by about 400 lines. The layer is now about 3\,600 lines, still importing nothing outside itself.}

\new{\emph{Not done.} \lean{U1.lean} and \lean{SU/Basic.lean} still each define their structure maps by hand (about 150 lines each); a further step would define the carriers themselves as subtypes of matrices and take the maps from \lean{MatrixJets}, but that changes the carrier types the Standard Model files unfold to, so it waits for the hand-built Standard Model code to go.}

\subsection*{Matter fields and species (PRs \#1989 and \#2011, 29 October--3 November 2026)}

\emph{Changed.} PR \#1989 (merged 30 October) added \lean{statistic : FieldStatistic} to \lean{MatterField}, and requires the fermionic and bosonic entries of the field data to have the matching statistic; merging fields with \lean{prod} or \lean{pi} now needs a common statistic as well as a common mass weight. PR \#2011 (opened 1 November) introduced \lean{inductive Species | matter M | gauge} with case-defined coefficients and gauge, weight, derivative and Lorentz laws, and moved the representation and gauge-field coefficients into \lean{MatterField/RepCoeff} and \lean{GaugeBoson/GaugeCoeff}.

\emph{Decided.} After discussion on 2 November the species type is dropped (see the decisions at the start). PR \#2011 is to be reworked: the coefficient extraction and the gauge-field composition laws are kept, the species type and its case-split API are not.

\emph{Now.} The field data is \lean{GaugeFieldData}, two indexed families of matter fields with the connection implicit; nothing depends on the species type.
